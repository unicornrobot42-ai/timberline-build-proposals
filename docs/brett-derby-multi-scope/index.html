The Project

A living estimate for seven scopes at 9 Emerald Glen.

This proposal covers seven distinct scopes across the home — from window shutters to a full bathroom gut remodel. Each is priced independently, and everything below reflects the scope as it stands today.

A "living estimate" means exactly that: as pricing comes together for items not yet priced — like the planter/retaining wall noted below — those get added to this total as new line items once the numbers are ready. Nothing here gets rebuilt from scratch; the total just grows as the picture fills in.

Timberline provides labor and construction materials. Finish materials — tile, fixtures, cabinets, slabs — are supplied by you unless a scope specifically states otherwise.

One clarification on what "you supply" actually means: it's about cost, not legwork. We often order, source, and coordinate delivery of these items on your behalf — "you supply" just means the cost of whatever you select isn't built into the price above. You're responsible for what it costs, not necessarily for tracking it down yourself.

Assumptions & What's Not Included

Site work like this always carries some unknowns until the ground is actually opened up. Here's exactly what this price assumes, and what would trigger an additional cost if conditions turn out differently.

  • Soil conditions: This price assumes normal, stable native soil. If we run into things like expansive soil, unstable fill, excess moisture, buried debris, old concrete, existing footings, large roots, or other unsuitable material, the extra excavation or remediation needed would be handled as a change order.
  • Excavation depth: Includes excavation and aggregate base to the depth we've planned for. If site conditions call for deeper excavation, additional base material, geogrid, or extra soil reinforcement, that's additional.
  • Drainage: Assumes we can maintain your existing grades while still achieving proper drainage. New area drains, channel drains, catch basins, dry wells, underground drainage piping, or major regrading aren't included unless specifically called out above.
  • Underground utilities: We're not responsible for irrigation, electrical, gas, water, sewer, drainage, low-voltage, or pool lines that aren't identified or properly marked beforehand. If we hit one, relocating or repairing it is additional.
  • Irrigation & landscaping: Doesn't include relocating or repairing irrigation, replacing landscaping, removing trees or roots, or modifying adjacent planters unless specifically listed above.
  • Steps & retaining conditions: Assumes the existing steps can be rebuilt without needing structural retaining walls, engineered foundations, or major changes to nearby structures. Any structural work discovered during demo would be additional.
  • Paver material allowance: This price carries an allowance for average-priced pavers. Upgrading to pricier pavers, special-order colors, porcelain pavers, or premium borders would add cost, which you'd cover.
  • Pattern & borders: This price assumes a standard running-bond/I-pattern layout with a standard border. Herringbone, multiple borders, inlays, contrasting bands, radius work, or other custom layouts cost more.
  • Existing concrete: Demo pricing assumes typical residential concrete thickness. Unusually thick concrete, double slabs, heavy reinforcement, structural concrete, or unusually difficult demo conditions would be additional.
  • Haul-off: Assumes normal haul-off quantities for the scope described. Extra soil or material generated by unexpected site conditions would be additional.
  • Permits, HOA & engineering: Permit fees, HOA submittal fees, surveying, and civil or soils engineering/testing are not included in this price.
  • City-required work: If the city requires changes to the public right-of-way, driveway apron, curb/gutter, sidewalk, or accessibility upgrades that aren't part of this scope, that work would be additional.
  • Site access: Assumes normal equipment access for demolition equipment, skid steer, and dump trucks/material delivery. If access is restricted and requires hand excavation or manual material movement, that's additional.
  • Matching existing materials: Existing stone, concrete, pavers, stucco, or landscaping disturbed during construction may not match exactly due to age, weathering, or discontinued products.
  • Grade elevations: Assumes your existing garage, entry, street, and adjacent landscape elevations allow the new paver system to go in without structural changes to the home, garage slab, retaining walls, or street improvements.
